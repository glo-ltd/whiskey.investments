Whiskey.Investments
Business model & landing page blueprint
Prepared for John Parnell · 11 June 2026
The model in one line. You run a digital reservation storefront for Great Northern Distillery
whiskey casks. Investors size their order with a calculator and pay a non-refundable 10%
deposit that is yours to keep as commission. The moment that deposit clears, the order and
the investor’s details pass straight to GND, who collect the 90% balance and make, store, certify
and resell the cask. You never touch the balance, never hold stock, and never make a sales
call.
1. How the money flows
The 10% reservation deposit is your margin; everything downstream is the distillery’s
responsibility. Because the casks are made to order, the deposit is non-refundable — stated
plainly in your terms and conditions and acknowledged at checkout.

3. Pricing & your unit economics
Casks are £2,000 each. They are bought by the crate — one crate is 6 casks (£12,000) — and
orders rise in whole crates, with a one-crate minimum. Your commission is always 10% of the
order value.
Volume discount — the more crates, the bigger the discount
Recommended method: a tiered crate discount. A single flat percentage is applied to the whole
order, set by the number of crates ordered. It is easy to understand, trivial to render live in the
calculator, and visibly rewards larger commitments. (An alternative — marginal discounting,
where only the crates above each threshold are discounted — is fairer at the margin but harder
for a first-time buyer to follow, so flat banding is the better front-end choice.)

5. Automation — watching deposits land without lifting a finger
Your only recurring job is confirming that deposits arrive and forwarding paid orders to GND.
That can be fully automated with a bank connector and a daily scheduled task:

1. Capture — Checkout collects every required detail plus the passport, assigns a unique
order reference, and takes the non-refundable 10% deposit. The investor pays using that
reference.
2. Read the bank — A daily scheduled task queries your bank/payment connector for new
incoming transactions and reads each payment reference.
3. Match — Each reference is matched to its open order in your records to confirm the
deposit has cleared.
4. Notify GND — For every newly-paid order, the task automatically sends GND the full
order pack and KYC, then marks the order “handed over” so it is never sent twice.
5. Daily digest — You receive a short daily digest of deposits received and orders forwarded
— read-only, no action needed.
Feasibility: this is buildable now. A GoCardless bank/payments connector is already available
to read incoming payments by date, and scheduled tasks can run the daily
reconciliation-and-notify routine. Once your checkout and order reference scheme exist, I can
set up the daily task for you.
6. Risks & compliance — read before you advertise
Advertising rules (ASA / CAP)
You are the advertiser, so the advertising liability is yours even though GND fulfils. Every page
and ad must state that the value of investments can go down as well as up, that whiskey cask
investment is not regulated in the UK, and must avoid guaranteeing returns or implying risk-free
/ early-retirement outcomes. Keep these disclaimers visible above the fold, not buried.
Non-refundable deposit
Because casks are made to order, the 10% deposit is non-refundable — set this out clearly in
the terms and conditions and require an explicit tick-box acknowledgement at checkout, before
payment. Have a lawyer confirm the wording works alongside UK consumer-contract rules so
the non-refundable term is enforceable.
KYC / AML & data
You collect a passport and full investor details at checkout, before the deposit, which makes you
a data controller for that data and potentially in scope for anti-money-laundering checks.
Confirm in the GND agreement who owns the KYC obligation, store documents securely, and
keep your privacy policy and GDPR basis tight.
Note: I’m not a lawyer or financial adviser. The points above flag where you need professional
advice before launch — they aren’t legal or financial advice themselves.
